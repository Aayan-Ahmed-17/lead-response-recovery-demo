# 15-Day Execution Brief

## Decision

Target **small home-service companies**—starting with plumbers, HVAC contractors, and electricians—in one reachable geography. Sell a **Lead Response Recovery Sprint**, not “AI services.” The sprint makes new inquiries visible, acknowledges them quickly, alerts the owner, and creates a human follow-up action using the company’s existing tools where possible.

This is the best first niche because the problem is revenue-adjacent and easy to observe publicly: a service website may have a form, phone number, or booking path, but the visitor experience often gives little clarity about what happens next. Home-service customers place importance on speed, communication, digital convenience, and follow-through, while the Federal Reserve reports that reaching customers and growing sales is the most common operational challenge among surveyed employer firms.[1] [2]

## Why not sell a chatbot

The current market is moving from experimentation toward workflow integration. The Federal Reserve found that only 7% of AI-using employer firms had fully integrated AI, with adapting tools to business needs a leading challenge.[1] JPMorganChase’s transaction-based analysis also shows that small-business AI spending has become more accessible and adoption has accelerated, while skills and integration gaps remain.[3] The commercial wedge is therefore implementation of one measurable workflow with human safeguards.

## Productized offer

The founding-client pilot is **$149 fixed price**, paid before work begins. It includes process mapping, a structured lead pipeline, immediate acknowledgement, owner alert, follow-up queue, five test submissions, and a handoff guide. It is limited to one inquiry workflow and does not include paid subscriptions, bulk outreach, automatic quoting, or guaranteed revenue results.

After a successful pilot, offer the **Quote Follow-Up Add-on** at **$99–$199** to create a simple, permission-based follow-up sequence for quoted work that has not received a reply.

## Acquisition channel ranking

| Rank | Channel | Use in the first 15 days | Reason |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Personalized direct outreach from public business listings and websites | Primary | Fast access to owner-operators; each message can cite a real observation and offer a small pilot |
| 2 | Local phone follow-up | Primary support | A short call can identify the person responsible for inquiries and avoid long email threads |
| 3 | LinkedIn | Secondary | Useful for owners and operations managers, but slower and dependent on profile access |
| 4 | Upwork | Selective | Public page showed 2,631 AI-automation jobs, but many visible examples required intermediate/expert experience and competition is high.[4] |
| 5 | Communities | Opportunistic | Use only where a request is already posted; never pitch into unrelated discussions |

## Funnel targets

The working target is 80 researched prospects, 50 personalized first touches, 15 follow-ups, 8 replies, 4 qualified conversations, 3 proposals, and 2 paid pilots. These are operating targets, not forecasts. If replies are weak after 25 well-researched contacts, change one variable—message, niche subsegment, or offer framing—before increasing volume.

## Delivery stack

The proof demo uses a static visual page plus Google Forms/Sheets/Gmail Apps Script. Apps Script is appropriate for low-volume, permission-based pilot workflows, but the official documentation lists account quotas and warns that limits can change.[5] n8n’s self-hosted Community edition is free indefinitely, but production hosting still needs to be arranged; it is a secondary option rather than a hidden promise of free hosting.[6]

## Immediate next actions

The proof assets are ready locally. The next execution phase is to obtain the sender identity and channel access needed for real outreach, then research 80 prospects in one selected geography, write one observation per prospect, and begin with 10 personalized contacts on day one. The first sales conversation should validate the current inquiry process before any proposal is sent.

## Geography decision addendum

### Selected market: Phoenix metro, Arizona, United States

The initial target geography is the Phoenix metropolitan area, using Mountain Standard Time (UTC−7). The choice is based on three practical factors: the August timing aligns with peak cooling-service urgency, the region has a visible concentration of HVAC and plumbing providers, and the time zone creates a workable overlap for an India-based solo operator during the prospect’s morning and early business hours.

ConsumerAffairs describes Phoenix as a highly demanding HVAC market because summer temperatures routinely exceed 110°F and its public directory shows multiple providers advertising same-day or one- to two-hour response times. This is directional directory evidence, not an independent market-size estimate.[7]

Google Maps redirected to a cookie-consent page in the sandbox, so prospect research will use alternative public sources such as provider websites, public directories, search-result listings, and LinkedIn pages. No data will be taken from private or login-only sources without the user’s account session.
